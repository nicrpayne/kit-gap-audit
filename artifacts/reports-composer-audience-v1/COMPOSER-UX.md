# Composer UX

## Interaction grammar

The Master Control Room supplies Signal's product identity: dark restrained materials, semantic cyan/violet/amber/mint/blue, explicit hierarchy, and modern instrument confidence. The FabFilter reference contributes dense-but-calm feedback and selected-module focus. Ableton's rack grammar contributes modular order, bypass-like active state, compact/expanded states, and presets.

Nothing is audio-themed: there are no knobs, fake hardware, gratuitous meters, neon decoration, or studio vocabulary.

## Surface model

1. **Transport** — audience, purpose, Reality/Scenario, comparison, and surface selector.
2. **Module browser** — visible active/inactive state and truth owner.
3. **Arrangement rack** — reorderable modules; the selected/expanded module receives emphasis while the whole brief remains legible.
4. **Inspector** — density, bindings, owner, as-of/currentness, permitted statements, removal, and ask promotion.
5. **Finished brief** — deliberately quieter, reading-first output.
6. **Site handoff preview** — progressive disclosure over the same snapshot/recipe.

At narrow widths the browser and inspector yield to the arrangement rather than shrinking controls into an expert-only strip.

## Progressive disclosure

Module headers always expose identity, density, and owner. Details may collapse in the Site-shaped surface, but caveats are kept available and the highest-priority modules open first. Print forces all included module bodies open.
