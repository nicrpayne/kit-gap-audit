# Signal by Role: Why You Care

Signal does not create different truths for different audiences. It gives each role a useful view of the same owner facts.

## Product Owner / PM

You care about accepted product shape, unresolved boundary choices, evidence, and readiness. Start with Audit, govern Capabilities in Scope, preserve real uncertainty in Decisions, and use Report Readiness before speaking for the project.

Signal's value: it separates “we heard this,” “we think this,” and “we have accepted this,” while keeping ticket execution connected to product intent.

## Delivery leader

You care about likely outcome, movement, constraints, leverage, and the specific ask. Start in Control Room; use Forecast for consequence, Portfolio for staffing what-ifs, Dependencies for propagation, and Reports for a frozen weekly brief.

Signal's value: when a date moves, you can inspect the modeled reason and ask a better intervention question than “can the team go faster?”

## Engineering lead

You care about accepted capability, executable work mapping, serial waits, dependency topology, and available capacity. Scope shows the gap between product shape and tickets; Decisions distinguishes important choices from actual delivery gates; Capacity avoids staffing-by-mention.

Signal's value: it refuses to collapse product intent, work items, people, and schedule into one ambiguous status number.

## Executive

You care about what changed, whether the project is likely to meet its target, what is committed, and where intervention is needed. Use the Executive report recipe, preserving coverage/currentness caveats and explicit leadership asks.

Signal's value: the brief is concise because the underlying traceability is deep, not because uncertainty was hidden.

## Stakeholder or partner

You care about the current outlook, movement, next milestone, and external dependency. Use the Stakeholder/Partner recipe. Avoid internal model mechanics unless they materially affect the agreement.

## Portfolio / staffing leader

You care about the complete roster, raw versus effective FTE, split allocations, dependency consequences, and opportunity cost. Use Portfolio Scenario first, then Forecast and the Portfolio/Staffing report recipe.

## Facilitator / meeting operator

Use the app like an instrument:

1. State the Reality baseline.
2. Name the question.
3. Move one Scenario lever.
4. Read the changed and unchanged outcomes.
5. Inspect why.
6. Return to Reality before the next question.

Never commit a live what-if merely because the room liked the result.
