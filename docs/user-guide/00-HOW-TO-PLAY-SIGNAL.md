# How to Play Signal

Signal is a governed delivery instrument. It turns incomplete project knowledge into an explicit operating picture, lets you test changes without pretending they are real, and recalculates delivery consequences from the truth you have accepted.

It is not a task tracker, a wiki, an AI-generated status page, or a place to record every new fact. Think of it as the point where knowledge becomes accountable delivery truth.

> **Changing Reality is governed. Calculating consequences is automatic.**

## 1. What Signal is

Signal answers five questions in a deliberate order:

1. **Reality:** What does the project currently accept as true?
2. **Choices:** What remains genuinely undecided, and which choices actually gate delivery?
3. **Capacity:** Who is available, where are they allocated, and what does switching cost?
4. **Outcome:** Given that state, where are we likely to land?
5. **Time:** What happened, what is planned, and what comes next?

The Master Control Room composes those answers. Owner instruments govern them. Audit finds disagreement between external intelligence and accepted Reality. Reports freeze an audience-specific story without changing the underlying facts.

### Why the neighboring tools are not enough

| Tool | What it is good at | What it cannot safely own |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Executable work, workflow state, parent/child work structure | Product intent with no ticket, organizational decisions, evidence provenance, named cross-project capacity, forecast meaning |
| KE/wiki | Institutional memory and synthesized current understanding | Governed delivery truth; a synthesis may be derivative and can be wrong or stale |
| Hermes | Longitudinal reasoning, contradiction discovery, synthesis, proactive artifacts | Authority to alter Scope, make a Decision, declare a Dependency, staff a team, or commit a target |
| Signal | The governed delivery model and its consequences | The raw knowledge corpus; Signal points upstream instead of becoming a second wiki |

Governed Reality matters because an observation is not a commitment. A meeting can reveal a likely capability, a possible dependency, or a tension that deserves a decision. Signal preserves that uncertainty until a human accepts the appropriate owner-specific change.

## 2. The mental model

### Three layers

| Layer | Meaning | Persistence |
|---|---|---|
| **Reality** | Saved canonical Scope, Decisions, Dependencies, Timeline events, named allocations, target, and live execution inputs | Persists until deliberately changed |
| **Scenario** | A hypothetical overlay on Reality: a staffing move, removed capability, assumed-resolved gate, or target scrub | Browser-local and disposable until a named commit action |
| **Forecast** | The modeled consequence of Reality, or Reality plus the active Scenario | Recomputed; never hand-typed |

Likely is the model's central outcome. Target is the date against which the model is evaluated. Commitment exists only when the owning record says it does. These words are not synonyms.

### The owner map

| Question | Owner | What other instruments may do |
|---|---|---|
| What is the product shape? | Scope | Read it and model its consequences |
| What is unresolved? | Decisions | Read it; Forecast sees only explicit serial DecisionGates |
| What waits on what? | Dependencies | Read declared edges and propagate consequences |
| Who is actually available? | Capacity / Portfolio | Forecast consumes effective reconciled capacity |
| Where does the model land? | Forecast | Control Room, Timeline, and Reports display it |
| What time objects are canonical? | Timeline | Other instruments link to them |
| What changed outside Reality? | Audit | Propose owner-specific mutations; never bypass the owner |
| What story was shared? | Reports | Freeze owner facts and presentation choices immutably |

## 3. Product laws

Keep these visible while operating:

- **Audit discovers. Owner instruments govern.** An Audit proposal is a candidate until accepted into the named owner.
- **Likely ≠ target ≠ committed.** A favorable forecast does not create a promise.
- **Live owner ≠ fresh/current.** A canonical read can still be stale.
- **Candidate/proposal ≠ Reality.** Machine intelligence stops at the governance boundary.
- **Semantic relation ≠ dependency.** Related concepts do not prove that one deliverable must wait for another.
- **Person mention ≠ staffing.** Only confirmed named allocations support a named-team claim.
- **Wiki synthesis ≠ independent evidence.** A derivative page does not multiply the evidence beneath it.
- **Modeled subset ≠ full-project forecast.** A computed date can be mathematically valid for incomplete work and still be unsafe as a project claim.
- **New evidence belongs upstream.** Put transcripts, correspondence, source documents, and synthesized knowledge in KE/Hermes; let Audit surface the delivery implications.

## 4. The operating loop

```text
MEETINGS / CORRESPONDENCE / SOURCE DOCUMENTS
                 ↓
       KE compiles institutional knowledge
                 ↓
   structured intelligence + evidence passages
                 ↓
     Hermes ingests, compares, and synthesizes
                 ↓
        Signal checks knowledge freshness
                 ↓
       Audit refresh + Changes since last Audit
                 ↓
      human accepts, defers, rejects, or routes
                 ↓
  Scope · Decisions · Dependencies · Capacity · Timeline
                 ↓
       Forecast recalculates consequences
                 ↓
      Control Room composes the live picture
                 ↓
       Reports freeze an audience-specific story
                 ↓
       interactive Site handoff, when supported
```

The top half turns information into proposals. The middle is the governance boundary. The bottom half calculates and communicates consequences.

## 5. Daily workflow — 10 to 15 minutes

1. Open **Audit** for the project.
2. Read the knowledge control before clicking anything:
   - **Knowledge · Current:** the latest completed package and snapshot agree.
   - **New intelligence available · Refresh:** a newer completed knowledge state exists.
   - **Ingestion in progress:** wait; Signal refuses partial Hermes state.
   - **Companion offline:** restore the local companion before refreshing.
3. Refresh only when the control says a completed update is available.
4. Open **Changes since last Audit**. For each proposal, compare current state, proposed state, evidence, currentness, and owner.
5. Accept only when the owner-specific consequence is correct. Defer when true but not ready; reject when wrong or out of scope. Information-only and source-health items never become delivery Reality.
6. Follow owner links for incomplete items. Do not fill missing IDs or dates merely to clear the inbox.
7. Open the **Control Room**. Scan Reality, Choices, Capacity, Outcome, and Time in order.
8. If the Control Room says the forecast is stopped or incomplete, follow the door to the owner instrument. Do not report through a caveat you have not understood.

Before leaving: know whether knowledge is current, which proposals remain, whether the forecast covers the full accepted shape, and what deserves a human decision.

## 6. Weekly workflow — 30 to 45 minutes

1. Complete the daily Audit loop.
2. In **Scope**, verify accepted capabilities and execution mapping. Treat **NO EXECUTION WORK MAPPED** and **NO CAPABILITY YET** as different problems.
3. In **Decisions**, review open choices. Connect one to delivery only if the work truly waits in series and you can supply the target Scope, reasoning, evidence, and low/likely/high resolution duration.
4. In **Dependencies**, inspect declared edges and downstream landings. Do not promote semantic similarity into topology.
5. In **Capacity**, verify the roster is still complete. Named exact means the complete roster is confirmed; it does not mean the people are productive on every ticket.
6. In **Forecast**, confirm coverage state, likely window, target confidence, and active Scenario state.
7. In **Timeline**, inspect Today, the live Forecast mark, planned events, commitments, and historical report snapshots.
8. Return to **Audit → Report Readiness**. Clear or consciously carry every blocker.
9. In **Reports**, choose audience and purpose, inspect the recipe, add only clearly labeled operator context, and create the immutable brief.

## 7. New project workflow

1. From the rail or command menu, choose **Add project**.
2. Enter a canonical name, aliases, owner hint, and source hints. Decide whether to search Signal-held knowledge or start blank.
3. Choose **Create & scan**. This creates pre-Reality identity only—not Scope, work, staffing, decisions, dependencies, milestones, Forecast, or Reality.
4. Watch the scan stages and provider coverage. A partial package is usable for review but remains partial.
5. Enter **Bootstrap Review**. Work section by section: Capabilities, Decisions, Dependencies, Sources, Missing information, and Ambiguities.
6. Inspect evidence in the Provenance Inspector. Derivative-only support is a warning, not a second proof.
7. Mark each candidate accepted, deferred, rejected, or information-only. At this stage, “accepted” means “include in the future activation manifest,” not “write canonical Reality now.”
8. Review the **Activation Manifest**. Confirm exactly what will become canonical and what will remain external/candidate.
9. In a disposable or intended real-project context, acknowledge provider gaps and activate. Activation is atomic and consequential.
10. Read the **First Audit**. Expect execution gaps; activation deliberately does not invent ticket links, gates, people, allocations, or milestones.
11. Configure execution mapping in Scope, reconcile Capacity, govern Decisions/Dependencies/Timeline, then let Forecast become available or honestly remain incomplete.

## 8. Existing project refresh workflow

1. Confirm the local companion is online and Hermes ingestion is complete.
2. In Audit, refresh only from a completed watermark/package.
3. Review the new immutable ContextSnapshot and the proposals produced by comparing it with accepted Reality.
4. Use project relevance labels to separate project-local facts, cross-scope relevance, neighboring context, and bleed.
5. Route each accepted proposal to its owner. An accepted capability writes Scope; an accepted milestone writes Timeline; an accepted Decision stays open and ungated; a Dependency requires explicit endpoints.
6. Re-open Scope, Forecast, Timeline, and Report Readiness to see the automatic consequences.

## 9. Report-readiness workflow

1. Start in **Audit → Report Readiness**, not Reports.
2. Check execution, accepted Scope mapping, capacity reconciliation, source health, knowledge/forecast currentness, and forecast coverage.
3. Follow each blocker link to the owner instrument.
4. When ready, open Reports from the readiness panel.
5. Select the project, audience, and purpose. Reorder or change module density only to improve communication; the facts remain frozen from owners.
6. Distinguish live preview from stored history. A saved report is immutable and carries generated-at/currentness context.
7. Use the Site handoff only when the interface presents it as supported. The handoff is user-mediated; Signal does not silently publish.

## 10. Scenario / what-if workflow

1. Begin from Reality and state the question: “What if James moves to iTrack?” is better than “make the date green.”
2. Change one lever first. Portfolio controls capacity/allocation; Scope controls the modeled release shape; Decisions can assume an existing gate resolved; Forecast can scrub an output-side target for evaluation.
3. Read the Scenario marker and Reality comparison. Look for effects on the selected project and dependent projects.
4. Ask why an unchanged project did not move; another constraint may dominate.
5. Discard to return to Reality. A target scrub is evaluative and is not automatically part of a capacity commit.
6. Commit only when the scenario represents the intended actual roster/allocation state and the complete actual team has been established.

## 11. Source-health workflow

1. Open Audit's Change Inbox and choose **Source health**.
2. Inspect Linear, Notion, Figma, companion, last package, and last Audit readings.
3. Separate three conditions:
   - unconfigured: no source is expected yet;
   - unavailable/offline: a configured source cannot be read;
   - stale: a read exists but is outside its freshness horizon.
4. Use **Open project setup** for configuration. Do not interpret a green provider badge as proof that the content is complete or true.
5. Re-run the upstream ingestion/companion path, then refresh Audit. Signal should never be used to paste in missing evidence as a substitute.

## 12. Safe experimentation and recovery

- Search, Trace, Inspector, project switching, layout modes, camera movement, report recipe preview, and Scenario changes are safe exploration.
- **Back to Reality** or **Discard** removes browser-local Scenario changes.
- Browser Back/Forward restores URL-owned project and selection context; verify the project label after navigating.
- If the forecast refuses, stop. A refusal is safer than a plausible number built from missing or cyclic inputs.
- If a write returns an error, re-read the owner screen before retrying. Activation and candidate acceptance are designed to converge on their existing result, but do not assume every control is idempotent.
- Never use production to manufacture a screenshot state.

## 13. What Signal will never do automatically

Signal will not:

- turn a meeting statement into governed Scope;
- declare a DecisionGate because a topic sounds important;
- convert semantic relatedness into a Dependency;
- staff a project from a person's name in a transcript;
- treat derivative wiki synthesis as independent corroboration;
- call a modeled subset the project's canonical delivery forecast;
- equate a likely date, target, and commitment;
- edit historical reports when Reality changes;
- publish an interactive Site without an explicit user-mediated handoff;
- become the repository for raw project knowledge.

## 14. Where to start when confused

Ask what kind of confusion you have:

- “What changed outside Signal?” → **Audit**
- “What are we actually shipping?” → **Scope**
- “Have we made the choice?” → **Decisions**
- “What must happen first?” → **Dependencies**
- “Who is actually available?” → **Capacity**
- “Where does the model land?” → **Forecast**
- “What happened or comes next?” → **Timeline**
- “What deserves attention now?” → **Control Room**
- “How do I brief someone?” → **Reports**
- “Where did this claim come from?” → **Search, Trace, Inspector**
- “How do I start with dormant knowledge?” → **Project Activation**

If none fits, the information probably belongs upstream in KE/Hermes.

## 15. Compact glossary

- **Accepted Reality:** canonical state written through an owner instrument.
- **Candidate/proposal:** a suggested change that has not crossed governance.
- **Currentness:** age of a source reading; independent of whether it is live or canonical.
- **DecisionGate:** an explicit, evidenced claim that a target Scope waits serially for a Decision.
- **Effective FTE:** raw named allocation after the shared context-switch model.
- **Forecast coverage:** whether modeled execution inputs represent the accepted project shape.
- **Likely:** central modeled landing outcome; not a target or promise.
- **Report snapshot:** immutable story generated from owner facts at a point in time.
- **Scenario:** disposable overlay on Reality.
- **Target:** date used to evaluate confidence; only canonical after an explicit owner write.
